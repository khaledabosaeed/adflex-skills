# Video types

What each type looks like when it works, taken from the team's finished videos. Use it to shape the script and the shot list. Timings are what the cuts actually used, not rules.

## Pick the type

| | `ad` | `social` | `hero` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Sell AdFlex with a paid-ad CTA | Earn organic reach; AdFlex is the sign-off | Show a product feature on the website |
| Format | 9:16, 24–48 s | 9:16, 13–25 s | 16:9, 17–20 s, plus a small social cut |
| Voice | Presenter, narrator or mascot, in dialect | No dialogue | None, or music only on the social cut |
| On screen | Real chat and UI recordings, captions | Almost no text | Typed prompts, big titles |
| Ends on | Brand end card with an approved line | A short AdFlex signature | Tagline, then end card on the social cut |
| Typical cost | 3,000–10,000 credits | 3,000–3,700 credits | 1,600–4,700 credits |

If the request doesn't say, ask which one. The type sets the folder (`videos/<type>/<slug>/`) and the reference list to check.

## Ad

**Structure that worked**

1. **Hook in the first 2 s:** a finished result, an impossible deadline, or a bold claim. The presenter talks from frame 1.
2. **Proof:** the product working. Real chat screens, a typed prompt, numbered step cards, a results grid.
3. **Payoff line** that names AdFlex first.
4. **CTA**, then the end card.

Shapes used so far:
- *Result first:* the finished ad plays, then "all I did was explain the idea", the live prompt, step cards, a recap, the creator on camera.
- *Mini-drama:* impossible deadline → AdFlex makes the whole campaign → results grid → "who did all this in one night?" → payoff.
- *Mascot narrator:* a character walks through locations and tells the story while small picture-in-picture screens show the real chat.
- *Duet:* a UGC ad opens the reel, then the presenter and the UGC character say the same line in split screen, followed by a 3-step walkthrough.

**Rules of thumb**

- One line and one action per talking clip. Change framing between talking shots.
- A fast montage sits well between two talking beats: 8 shots of about 0.3 s, punch-in zoom, the ads' own sound.
- Pop-up cards around a presenter must stay clear of the face. Measure the face box first.
- Screen demos float as a rounded box over the presenter's chest, with the full original take behind. This replaced green-screen cutouts.
- Step cards hold about 1 s each; typing runs about 0.2 s per word.
- The spoken answer, the captions and the end card all put AdFlex first. A cut where the partner AI is named last and loudest reads like that partner's ad.

**Good ad references:** a competitor or nearby AI tool clearly running the video as a paid ad, a hook inside 2 s, the product shown working, a clear payoff and CTA.

## Social

**Structure that worked**

1. **Cold open on the strongest gag.** No title, no text.
2. **Escalating gags,** one per shot, each topping the last. The joke must work with the sound off.
3. **Poster shot,** slowed slightly, with the AdFlex signature landing on a beat.

**Rules of thumb**

- Cut on the music's real beat grid, every 2 beats (about 1.2 s at 99 BPM). Measure the grid; don't guess it.
- End just after the track's final hit. A silent tail reads as a mistake.
- Generate 3 s per shot when the cut uses about 1.2 s.
- One sound effect on the hit of each gag (snarl, glass, cash machine).
- A recurring character and a recurring companion make it a series.
- A consistent look carries it: one film grade across every shot (for example faded 1990s 35 mm with grain).
- On-screen text close to zero. Only invented or logo-free brands.

**Good social references:** high organic reach, a joke that survives being recast in a Gulf or Levantine setting, a premise that can become a series, and gags the product can live inside.

## Hero

**Structure that worked**

1. **Title** over a moving plate (about 2 s).
2. **Prompt beat, then result beat,** repeated: a typed prompt in the real AdFlex UI (1.05–1.6 s), then the object it changes (1.45–1.95 s).
3. **Tagline** on the last result. The website cut loops silently; the social cut adds a 2 s end card and music.

**Rules of thumb**

- Hard cuts. No zoom-blur whips, white flashes or camera shake between shots.
- Show the object plain first (about 0.4 s), then the change. Nothing transforms before the trigger lands.
- One travelling element (a logo or spark leaving the Generate button) carries continuity from shot to shot: it exits one frame and enters the next from the opposite side.
- Titles type on character by character. Prompts type left to right in English and right to left in Arabic.
- It must read with no sound.
- Speed plates up 1.2–1.8× when the pacing drags; a 24 s cut became 17 s that way.
- Use the brand purple, not a pastel version of it.

**Good hero references:** a polished AI-product launch that shows the output rather than a person talking, bold motion graphics, and a clean silent loop.

## Reference distance

A reference gives the idea, structure and motion style. A version whose story matched its reference too closely was rejected and remade with a different setting and plot. When the script could be described with the same one-line summary as the reference, change the story.
